# Assault-rifle refinement diagnosis v2

The current render is recognizable as an AR-pattern rifle, but it is not yet showcase quality. The dominant failure is not only exposure: the receiver is a dark undifferentiated mass, the stock reads as a sparse brace instead of a convincing collapsible assembly, the optic lacks a clearly modeled mount and rear housing, the handguard’s blue pattern is strong but overly uniform, the magazine lacks a readable feed-lip/baseplate/profile treatment, and the barrel/muzzle silhouette is too generic.

The next refinement should preserve the long shallow source silhouette while adding visually legible, separately named hard-surface subassemblies: upper/lower receiver seam and bevel bands, ejection-port recess with dust cover, bolt catch and pins, trigger/guard silhouette, charging handle, buffer tube and stock latch, optic mount/hood/window frame, handguard top rail and front cap, vent-slot interiors with edge bevels, magazine ribs/feed lips/baseplate, foregrip clamp and tread, gas block/front sight, barrel collar, and a multi-slot muzzle brake with visible bore.

Material treatment should become more photographic and less like one emissive blue band. Keep charcoal as the base, but introduce controlled blue-violet/teal marbling at multiple scales, a warmer edge-wear response, roughness separation between coated metal, polymer, and cavity surfaces, and brighter neutral review lighting. The dark reference route should remain available, but the quality gate must use a readable neutral/grazing-light route for geometry inspection.

## First refinement capture

The revised factory now reports 189 meshes instead of 134, and the neutral broadside visibly gains stock buttpad ridges, receiver controls/relief, optic framing, magazine/handguard sub-detail, and muzzle-cage structure. The main remaining weakness is still presentation contrast: the receiver and polymer parts are readable only as deep silhouettes under the current neutral route, while the marbling reads as a dark violet band. The next capture will use the source-matched route and then a brighter inspection route to calibrate lighting without flattening the material response.

## Brighter neutral capture v17

The brighter inspection route materially improves separation: the blue-violet/teal marbling now has multiple visible filaments, the stock rear ridges and receiver/optic outlines are easier to read, and the handguard vent/rail cadence is stronger. The dark receiver still needs a distinct upper/lower plane break and the optic window/reticle remains the brightest focal point; this is acceptable for the next orbit review, but the final reference route should retain controlled contrast rather than returning to near-black.

## Receiver and stock ornamentation capture v18

The receiver and stock now carry the same controlled marbled treatment as the handguard, making the model feel intentionally designed rather than unfinished. The mesh count is 194. The receiver panel is visible but still slightly too dark relative to the handguard; the next validation is an orbit view to confirm the added panels are shallow overlays rather than floating geometry and that the muzzle cage/bore read from depth.

## Three-quarter orbit capture v19

The orbit confirms the refinement is volumetric rather than a broadside card: the optic hood, receiver depth, vented handguard shell, magazine sidewall, foregrip clamp, gas tube, muzzle cage, and bore all hold depth. The key remaining visual weakness is that the three-quarter route uses the dark presentation rig, so the lower receiver and stock internals merge into shadow. Geometry is now substantially stronger; the final review should capture the same orbit under a brighter neutral/grazing-light variant and retain the dark route only as a look-development view.

## Hemisphere-fill orbit capture v21

The added hemisphere fill improves the lower receiver and magazine sidewall separation without washing out the marbled handguard. The orbit continues to show stable depth and no floating panel overlays. The remaining issue is mostly artistic: the current coating is high-contrast violet and the body remains very dark, so the final render set should include both the readable neutral route and a restrained source-matched route rather than relying on one exposure.

## Top and side orbit captures v22-v23

The top view confirms the handguard is a thick shell with deep vent openings, top rail teeth, lower rail structure, optic mount, and a visible front cap. The side view confirms a real depth envelope rather than a billboard and shows the optic/receiver/handguard stack, magazine thickness, foregrip, barrel, and muzzle assembly in profile. The side view is intentionally a construction diagnostic and not a reference-matched composition; the broadside and bright three-quarter views remain the primary presentation evidence.

## Revised comparison-sheet assessment

The revised sheet is a clear improvement over the original render: the receiver and stock now carry intentional ornament panels, the handguard marbling has more scale variation, the stock has denser mechanical detail, and the overall silhouette is more complete. The main remaining source mismatch is presentation: the reference is a brighter studio-like black-background product image, while the neutral render uses a blue-gray background and a darker charcoal body. The procedural review should retain this limitation explicitly, but the model geometry is now substantially more detailed and volumetric.
