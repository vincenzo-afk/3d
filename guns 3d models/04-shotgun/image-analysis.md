# 04 Shotgun — Image Analysis

## Layer 1 — Identification and classification

The reference is a pump-action shotgun shown in a single broadside view. It is a long mechanical firearm with a wooden shoulder stock, dark metal receiver and dual-tube fore-end/barrel system, a wooden ribbed pump, trigger guard, loading/ejection opening, front sight, and a stepped muzzle assembly. Confidence in the visible broadside classification is high; backside and internal action details remain unobserved.

## Layer 2 — Overall form and silhouette

The object has a long horizontal bounding volume with a compact rear stock at the proximal end and a narrow forward barrel system terminating in a short muzzle device. The stock is an asymmetric extruded organic profile with a buttpad, cheek contour, grip transition, and lower drop. The receiver is a rounded rectangular metal shell. The forward system contains a thin upper barrel, a lower tubular magazine, a wood pump sleeve around the lower tube, a front support collar, and a stepped muzzle cap. The broadside is approximately bilateral in silhouette but materially asymmetric because of the ejection port and visible hardware.

## Layer 3 — Macro, meso, and micro decomposition

Macro assemblies are the stock, receiver/action, trigger group, upper barrel, lower magazine tube, pump fore-end, front barrel/magazine band, muzzle, and sighting elements. Meso structures include the buttpad and sling loop, stock cheek and grip checkering, receiver ejection port and side pins, trigger guard and trigger, pump rib bands and engraved end motifs, barrel/magazine support band, muzzle collar and knurled cap, and front sight post. Micro structures include repeated pump ribs, small receiver fasteners, wood grain/roughness variation, checkering facets, action seam, sight notch, and knurling.

## Layer 4 — Spatial relationships

The stock is attached to the rear receiver through an overlapping butt joint and a continuous grip/receiver transition. The trigger guard is embedded below the rear receiver and surrounds the trigger opening. The ejection port is flush-with and inset into the receiver side. The upper barrel is attached to the receiver front and runs above the lower tubular magazine. The pump fore-end is a sleeve surrounding and sliding along the lower magazine tube, positioned below the upper barrel. The front band overlaps both tubes and supports the sight region. The muzzle collar and knurled cap terminate the forward tube system. The buttpad is flush with the stock rear, and the sling loop is attached below the rear stock.

## Layer 5 — Materials and surface

The stock, grip, and pump are opaque medium-dark brown wood with visible directional grain, satin roughness, darker edge wear, and carved/checkered relief. The receiver, barrels, tube bands, and muzzle are opaque dark blued metal with low-to-medium roughness and narrow cool highlights. The trigger, pins, receiver fasteners, and band edges are polished or semi-polished dark metal. The buttpad and pump rib valleys are near-black matte rubber or recessed wood. The ejection port is a dark cavity with a raised metal lip.

## Layer 6 — Color and finish

The wood regions are warm brown with darker reddish-brown grain and near-black checkering. The metal regions are charcoal to blue-black with controlled silver-blue edge highlights. The image uses a light neutral background but the object itself contains deep values, so a neutral inspection route is required in addition to the source-matched route. The overall finish is satin wood plus blued/polished metal, not a flat single-color material.

## Layer 7 — Identity-defining features

The defining features are the pump-action architecture, the separate upper barrel and lower tubular magazine, the long ribbed wooden pump sleeve, the large dark receiver with ejection port, the warm carved stock and grip, the circular trigger guard, the stepped muzzle and knurled end cap, the front sight post, and the repeated carved triangular motifs at the pump ends and grip. These must receive explicit feature review targets.

## Layer 8 — Uncertainty and single-image limits

The far side, internal bolt/carrier, magazine follower, exact stock checkering topology, pump-to-tube clearance, and muzzle bore interior are hidden or partially occluded. The exact barrel wall thickness and the precise internal trigger/action mechanism are uncertain. The broadside camera is close to orthographic, but a small amount of perspective and lighting distortion is possible.

## Intake decision

Admit as a conditional but strong broadside reference. Use an orthographic source-matched route for silhouette and a neutral-light route for geometry, material separation, and orbit construction. Do not use the near-black object/background segmentation as the sole quality decision.
